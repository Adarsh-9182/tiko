Rebuilding Clicky, the AI buddy that lives next to your cursor, for zero cost.

Hold a shortcut, ask out loud. It looks at your screen, answers by voice, and flies over to point at the button you need.

Gemini free tier, on-device speech, macOS voices. No Xcode.

Part by part:

---

Part 1 — hearing.

A listen-only keyboard tap watches for ⌃⌥ in any app. It can see keys, never block them.

A third key while you hold it means ⌃⌥→ switching Spaces, not you talking, so recording cancels.

Speech is recognised on-device, in Indian English, for Hinglish.

---

Part 2 — seeing.

The screenshot is taken the moment you let go, in parallel with finishing the transcript. That is the screen you were asking about.

Tiko's own windows are left out, and a sharp close-up around the cursor goes along for "what's this?"

---

Part 3 — pointing.

Gemini ends its reply with a tag like [POINT:88,309:Appearance] on a 0–1000 grid.

A coordinate that comes back slightly off-screen is clamped, not dropped.

Then a 400-point crop around the guess gets checked again before the buddy flies.

---

Part 4 — stopping it.

Clicky's issue tracker has one titled "It doesn't stop once it starts speaking".

In Tiko, Esc stops the voice, the request and the pointing at once. Asking something new cuts the old answer off too.

---

Part 5 — tasks that take several clicks.

It can't list every step up front: step 3's menu item isn't on screen until step 2 opens the menu.

So it gives one step, points at it, and waits. Tap the shortcut or say "next" and it looks at the screen again.

---

Part 6 — measuring instead of hoping.

A benchmark draws 6 Mac screens in code — Settings, a toolbar, a login page, a menu, a dialog, a spreadsheet — and records the exact rectangle of every button.

29 Hinglish questions, asked twice, through the app's own pointing path.

---

First run: every "no point" answer was actually a point.

Gemini wrote [POINT:185,119,filter button] with a comma where the prompt asked for a colon, and the parser silently threw it away.

The same bug was in the app. One benchmark run found it.

---

Second finding: on System Settings the height was right and the x was way off.

The model was measuring on the cursor close-up instead of the full screen.

Labelling the close-up with the part of the screen it shows took the first guess from 55% to 81%.

---

The honest part of that result.

The full path, first guess plus the zoom check, stayed flat: 84% → 83%. The fix moved accuracy into the first guess.

And the spreadsheet screen got worse, 10 → 6 of 12.

That is why it gets measured.

---

Then a fix that needs no second request: when the model names a label, read the screen on the Mac and snap to that text.

Labels found: 27. On target: 27.

Whole path: 83%, with 1.53 requests per question instead of 2.

---

Status, honestly:

6,246 lines of Swift, 93 checks, 14 pull requests, one zip for Apple silicon and Intel.

A typed question has made it through the running app to an answer beside the cursor. But nobody has held the shortcut and asked it something for real yet.

That is next.
